# Brief: Individual Research Paper

## Research Question

Under what conditions does a nurse residency program generate a positive return
on investment for a U.S. nonprofit community hospital facing ongoing nurse
turnover costs?

## The Challenge

Many U.S. hospitals face persistent workforce and retention challenges despite
growth in the registered nurse (RN) workforce. Nurse turnover remains costly
because replacing experienced nurses requires recruitment, onboarding, training,
overtime, and temporary staffing expenditures. At the average U.S. hospital,
staff RN turnover was 17.6% in 2025, and each departure cost approximately
$60,090 to replace (NSI, 2026).

This is both an economic and operational problem because turnover increases
labor costs, disrupts staffing stability, and may negatively affect patient
outcomes and care quality (Aiken et al., 2002). Hospital leaders must therefore
determine whether investing in nurse retention programs produces greater value
than repeatedly replacing departing nurses.

## Economics It Touches

Microeconomics

* Labor supply elasticity: whether RN employment responds strongly or weakly to
  wage increases.
* Turnover costs: hospitals incur recruitment, onboarding, overtime, temporary
  staffing, and productivity costs when nurses leave.
* Cost-benefit analysis and incentives: hospitals must decide whether a
  retention program's benefits exceed its costs, compared with repeatedly
  replacing departing nurses.

## Planned Analysis

This paper will examine the following causal chain:

Inelastic RN supply → turnover costs → nurse residency program → financial return

The analysis will:

1. Measure the observed ratio of % change in RN employment to % change in the
   real median RN wage (BLS OEWS, May 2015–May 2019, deflated by CPI-U), shown
   in Figure 1. This is consistent with inelastic supply if below 1.0, but it
   is not a precise elasticity because supply and demand may both have shifted
   over the window. **Result: the ratio is about 12.7, so this link fails.**
   The paper reports that this measure does not show inelastic supply. Links 2
   and 3 do not depend on it.
2. Measure turnover and its cost: NSI RN turnover rates over 2023–2025 and NSI
   cost per RN turnover ($60,090 in the 2026 report). **Result: turnover fell
   0.8 points (18.4% → 17.6%), so this link fails on trend.** The analysis
   relies on the 17.6% level, because the ROI depends on the level of turnover,
   not its trend.
3. Evaluate the nurse residency program: the new-graduate transition-to-practice
   program studied by Silvestre et al. (2017). It is the one strategy computed
   in full because it has a published cost and effect that can be moved to a
   single hospital. Wage increases, bonuses, and working-condition changes are
   discussed as alternatives but not computed because none has a comparable
   evaluation.
4. Move the program to the case hospital: avoided new-graduate departures ×
   $60,090, compared with the program's cost for the hospital's annual
   new-graduate hires, on full cost and on ongoing cost only (what a hospital
   adopting an existing program would pay).
5. Recompute the ROI with the published effect reduced by 50%, and find the
   break-even share of the published effect on each cost basis, shown in
   Figure 2.

The paper is written for the chief financial officer of a U.S. nonprofit
community hospital with about 490 RNs (the average hospital in the NSI 2026
report), deciding whether to fund a nurse retention program.

The paper ends with a conditional recommendation to the CFO: the share of the
published effect the hospital must achieve for the program to pay for itself.

## What Would Prove Me Wrong

These conditions were set before any data were pulled. My argument would be
weakened if the data show that:

* The observed employment–wage ratio is 1.0 or higher.
* Staff RN turnover rises by less than 1.0 percentage point between 2023 and
  2025.
* The nurse residency program's annual cost exceeds the annual turnover cost it
  avoids (ROI < 1.0).

Results against each condition are recorded in the spec.
