**Your spec is still the template, and it is item 1 — most of what follows is work you do inside it.**
`capabilities/economic-research/spec.md` is 498 bytes and still carries
`<!-- PLACEHOLDER: Write this after the brief, before drafting. -->` above four empty headings. The
brief moved and the spec did not. Its four questions are the right four — data sources and provenance,
the mechanism, the figure, the success criteria — and answering them is the cheapest useful hour on
this paper.

**The brief is wider than four pages.** Nine economic mechanisms across micro and macro, five analysis
strands, and four pages excluding title page, graphs, bibliography and appendix. The assignment's own
guidance is that a challenge narrow enough to put a number on beats a sweeping one. Which single
relationship is the paper? Staffed versus licensed inpatient beds driving boarding hours is the one
your strands keep returning to, and the one with a number at both ends.

**Your falsification section is real, and it is the thing to protect.** "ED throughput is improving
despite staffing shortages" and "the mismatch ... is temporary rather than structural" would change
your conclusion, and you wrote them before the data. To fire, each needs a series and a threshold in
the spec — improving on which measure, over what period, by how much — or they become conditions you
can talk past.

**No series has been pulled.** `data/` holds only its README, which asks for source, date pulled and
transformations. Which ED throughput measure is currently published at hospital level, and where do
staffed-versus-licensed bed counts come from for your sample? The crowding-mortality link I would take
from a peer-reviewed source rather than a database — you are borrowing that estimate, not measuring
it. Every number in the paper has to appear in a committed artifact, and starting that way is cheap.

**Graph/Chart/Diagram is its own ten percent, and a table does not satisfy it.** What does yours plot?
The staffed-bed gap against boarding hours, on one pair of axes, is the candidate your brief already
implies. Name it in the spec before it becomes whatever the data happens to allow.

**Recommendation is thirty percent, and the brief has no decision-maker.** It ends on determining
whether labor-market conditions can improve throughput — that is a finding. Who acts on it: a hospital
operations leader choosing between overtime, travel nurses and closing a zone; a state legislature
weighing ratio rules; a payer? The audience decides which results you foreground, and seventeen years
from CNA to quality and patient safety is a vantage point most papers on this topic do not have.

**Is the claim international, or is the US your case?** The assignment asks for a global challenge and
the brief is scoped to Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement and national labor-force participation.
Say which in the brief; Content and Relevance is twenty percent and it is decided there. If the
analysis later answers a different question than the brief states, there are two honest exits and no
third: amend the brief and spec so the paper's question is the stated question, or write to the
question you specified. Amending is the sanctioned move, and the history records it.

**Mechanical, while it is cheap.** Citations in APA, MLA or Chicago with a separate bibliography page.
Peer review is double-anonymous: your name on the title page only, no identifying information on any
body page, no repository URL anywhere in the paper. Those and the page limit sit under timeliness and
adherence to constraints, a separate twenty-five percent from the criteria above.
