# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant **10 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mesclun**. 
This mix earns the highest profit under current land and labor limits.

## Why
Tomatoes stop at 10 beds because the 11th bed would lose money: marginal cost jumps from **$8,249** at bed 10 to **$9,391** at bed 11, above the **$8,800** price. 
Carrots and mesclun, by contrast, remain profitable all the way to their caps. Carrot marginal cost at bed 20 is **$1,688.95** against a **$2,094** price, and mesclun’s bed 30 cost is **$2,420.10** against a **$2,700** price. Both crops are still earning money when they hit their limits.

## The judgment call
Both the carrot and mesclun caps bind, but carrot land is worth more to relax. A 21st carrot bed would earn about **$352.49**, while a 31st mesclun bed would earn **$246.47**. 
If expansion is possible, carrot ground should be purchased first. Total land and temp labor do not bind — the plan uses only 60 of 64 beds and 3.16 of 4 temp workers — so relaxing those constraints would not change the crop mix.

## What would change my answer
A significant drop in tomato price — for example, **a 20% decline** — would reduce tomato acreage and shift the optimal mix.
